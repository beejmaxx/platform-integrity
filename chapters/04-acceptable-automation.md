# 4. Acceptable Automation

<p class="chapter-subtitle">Design a policy spectrum that preserves beneficial automation while constraining harm.</p>

<div class="chapter-meta"><span>Status: brief</span><span>Part II</span><span>Decision: what to permit</span></div>

## Reader outcome

Classify automation as allowed, allowed with limits, discouraged, restricted, or prohibited using ecosystem consequences rather than technical detectability.

## Scope

Accessibility, APIs, scripts, macros, testing, mods, collection, third-party clients, AI assistants, and commercial automation.

## Decision questions

- Which capabilities should have an authorized path, quota, or disclosure requirement?
- Is the rule understandable and consistently enforceable?
- Does a restriction burden accessibility or interoperability disproportionately?
- How will policy survive new tools that produce the same behavior differently?

## Evidence needed

- Comparative platform policies and developer programs
- Accessibility standards and user research
- Examples of safe harbors, quotas, and approved automation
- Enforcement consistency and policy-change histories
