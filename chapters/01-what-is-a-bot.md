# 1. What Is a Bot?

<p class="chapter-subtitle">Choose a governable category without confusing automation, intent, identity, and harm.</p>

<div class="chapter-meta"><span>Status: brief</span><span>Part I</span><span>Decision: what to classify</span></div>

## Reader outcome

Distinguish automation from prohibited conduct and decide whether “bot” is useful for a specific platform policy or technical system.

## Scope

Compare software agents, human-assisted automation, macros, accessibility tools, test clients, scrapers, farming systems, purchasing agents, modified clients, and AI agents. Test definitions against benign and mixed-use counterexamples.

## Decision questions

- Is the relevant object a tool, behavior, outcome, actor, or coordinated operation?
- Does intent matter, and can the platform observe it reliably?
- Which legitimate automation would a binary bot label collapse?
- Can policy be expressed in observable conduct rather than implementation detail?

## Evidence needed

- Platform policies using competing definitions
- Technical literature on automated agents and human-in-the-loop systems
- Accessibility and authorized API counterexamples
- Case law or regulatory definitions where consequential

## Open tension

A broad definition improves coverage but weakens legitimacy and precision; a narrow technical definition is easy to evade and may miss the actual harm.
