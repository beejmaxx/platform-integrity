# 3. The Economics of Abuse

<p class="chapter-subtitle">Model defense as a contest over expected value, capacity, and replacement cost.</p>

<div class="chapter-meta"><span>Status: brief</span><span>Part I</span><span>Decision: where to change incentives</span></div>

## Reader outcome

Estimate attacker profit and platform loss well enough to choose between prevention, friction, containment, and resilience.

## Starting models

```text
Expected attacker profit = revenue - accounts - infrastructure - labor
                         - detection losses - replacement cost

Expected platform loss = direct loss + customer harm + support + infrastructure
                       + reputation + regulatory risk
```

## Decision questions

- Which costs are marginal, fixed, transferable, or subsidized?
- Does defense reduce profit, limit scale, increase variance, or merely displace abuse?
- Can attackers externalize false-positive costs onto legitimate users?
- What is the defender's sustainable cost per prevented unit of harm?

## Evidence needed

- Measured abuse-market prices and replacement cycles
- Intervention experiments and displacement effects
- Defender operating costs and customer-friction costs
- Economic models with stated assumptions rather than invented precision
